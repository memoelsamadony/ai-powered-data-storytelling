The pertussis incidence rate stayed mostly between about 20 and 40 cases per million from 2000 until 2019. During these years, the number of reported cases also stayed in a fairly stable range, usually between about 117000 and 250000.

The rate then dropped sharply in 2020 to 9.9 per million and decreased even further in 2021 to only 4.6 per million. The number of cases in 2021 was about 30000, making it the lowest year in the whole record.

After this low point, pertussis started rising again. The rate reached 9 per million in 2022 and 22.7 in 2023. Then, at 2024, it witnessed a massive spike and hit 137.1 per million with about 942000 cases globally, which is the highest value by far.

At 2025, both values decreased sharply. Cases fell to about 265000 and the incidence rate reached 37.3 per million.

Overall, pertussis stayed relatively stable for many years, dropped heavily around 2020 and 2021, and then witnessed a very unusual spike in 2024 before coming back down in 2025.
